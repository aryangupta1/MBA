The thesis the whole piece defends

Core argument

Carry this thread through all six sections

QS5's long-term value to 2050 will be determined not by model accuracy but by the maturity of the governance architecture around it. Anticipatory stewardship is achievable but not default — it depends on decisions made now about co-governance, explainability and workforce transition. Reliability is a governance obligation, not a model-performance target.

ForecastImagineTransform

Three families of futures methods. Your work lives in Imagine (scanning, signals, scenarios) and Transform (backcasting, SLOs) — deliberately not Forecast alone.

The Futures Cone

possibleplausibleprobablepreferable

Classifies futures by likelihood & desirability (Voros, 2003). Your four scenarios sit on the plausible band; backcasting targets the preferable future.

Thesis to echo in §1: the shift “from prediction to preparation / meaning-making” — the “map vs compass” idea (Modules 6 & 9). Foresight engages plural futures, not one forecast.

Citation cautions

Three things to get right

  • Don't name the lecturers. The Module 8/9 speakers aren't named in the transcripts. Cite lecture ideas as the module (e.g. DMBA6002 Module 8, 2026); cite Schoemaker / Schwartz / Selin / Wack separately as external support — never as your lecturers.
  • F.I.T. is the unit's own framework. Cite it as a Module 6 concept; don't invent an external author. The Futures Cone is correctly Voros (2003).
  • Verify every reference. Page ranges, DOIs and volumes were reconstructed from standard records — check each, and trim to only what you cite (minimum 20).
