Prep — Alstonville Limited case

Live Session

Purpose of the exercise

  • Apply capital budgeting principles to ambiguous, contested investment assumptions, rather than simply calculating NPV from accepted inputs
  • Decide whether individual cash-flow items should be included, excluded, or adjusted based on whether they are genuinely incremental
  • Defend those decisions when adopting different executive or managerial perspectives
  • The session will emphasise on project evaluation requiring financial judgement and challenge, rather than pure spreadsheet mechanics

Alstonville Limited

Strategic Context

Alstonville is a long-established consumer goods company responding to digital disruption through a proposed $30m digital transformation

The initiative includes:

  • AI powered e-commerce
  • Personalised digital marketing
  • Real-time demand and inventory analytics
  • Integration with third party delivery and subscription services

The strategic rationale is the modernisation of Alstonville’s commercial and customer interface, but the financial case must establish whether these initiatives actually generate incremental economic value

Alstonville’s historical investment-evaluation problem

Previous business cases captured obvious revenues and expenses but often missed less visible incremental effects:

  • Opportunity costs from redeploying assets/resources
  • Internal resource requirements
  • Working-capital changes
  • Cannibalisation of existing revenue

Prior marketing automation and logistics projects relied on overly optimistic assumptions and failed to distinguish

Value genuinely caused by the project ≠ Value that would occur anyway

Consequently, forecast returns were inflated and disappointing outcomes were later attributed to “execution challenges”, even where the original business case itself was flawed

Key diagnostic: poor realised returns may originate in bad investment assumptions not merely poor implementation

The Governance Problem: Persuasion versus Evaluation

Alstonville’s investment decisions have also been distorted by executive influence over the CFO and finance team.

Executive PerspectiveTypical ArgumentFinancial risk / challenge
Head of Strategy“Future-proof the business”Strategic narrative may be strong while incremental cash-flow logic is weak
Head of Brand & MarketingCustomer engagement and intangible brand benefitsBenefits may be overstated without evidence on conversion, cost-to-serve or cash impact
Head of OperationsEfficiency, supply chain and asset utilisationCapita-intensive proposals may improve operations without sufficient customer or economic value
CFO / FinanceFinancial evaluation and challengeMust remain independent rather than allowing dominant executives to determine assumptions
  • Different functions legitimately value different outcomes, but those perspectives cannot replace consistent project-evaluation principles
  • Without a disciplined framework, capital budgeting becomes a contest of internal influence rather than an assessment of economic value

Applying the Incremental Cash-Flow Principle

Include a cash flow only if it changes because the project proceeds

The analysis should ask:

QuestionWhat it tests
Would this happen without the project?“Incrementality”
Are existing assets/resources being redeployed?Opportunity cost
Does the initiative require additional inventory, receivables or other funding?Working capital
Will new digital sales replace existing channel sales?Cannibalisation
Is an internal change a real additional cash cost or merely an allocationIncremental overhead
Is the claimed benefit measurable and attributable to the project?Validity of benefits
Is management presenting a strategic argument rather than a cash-flow consequence?Assumption discipline

Role-Bases Perspectives and Ambiguity

  • Some scenarios may reasonably produce different initial recommendations depending on the executive role adopted
  • The purpose is not to eliminate strategic disagreement, but to distinguish:
    • Legitimate differences in strategic perspective from Incorrect financial treatment of project cash flows
  • Management assumptions should therefore be challenged for both:
    • Their economic relevance
    • The evidence supporting their magnitude

Analytical Takeaways

  • Capital budgeting is partly a governance mechanism: it forces strategic claims to be translated into attributable, incremental economic consequences
  • A persuasive strategy narrative does not justify an investment unless its assumptions survive scrutiny around cash flow, opportunity cost, working capital, cannibalisation and risk
  • The CFO’s role is not simply to calculate management’s preferred assumptions; finance must independently challenge whether those assumptions belong in the model at all
  • Post-project underperformance should trigger examination of both execution and original business-case assumptions, particularly where forecast benefits were weakly attributable

In ambiguous investment decisions, the strongest argument is the one that can connect managerial judgement back to the incremental cash-flow principle, rather than executive authority or functional performance
