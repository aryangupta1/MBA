Gilead Investment Case

Gilead is considering two six-year technology investments as part of its digital transformation

Initiative A - AI PersonalisationInitiative B - Micro-Fulfilment Centres
Strategic purposeImprove personalisation, conversion, order value and retentionImprove omni-channel fulfilment speed and reduce logistics costs
Initial investment$20m$35m
Cash-flow profileStable: $6m p.a.Higher initially, then declining
Key implementation riskData integration and frontline adoptionCapital intensity and operational complexity
Hurdle rate supplied10%10%

Initiative B is described as having greater upfront risk and complexity, yet the exercise applies the same 10% hurdle rate to both. 10% should be used for the required calculations, but this assumption is worth questioning in discussion

Cash Flows

Initiative A

(-20,6,6,6,6,6,6)

  • $20m cash outflow at Year 0
  • $6m inflow each year for six years
  • Cash flows are relatively stable and predictable

Initiative B

(-35,12.25,11.25,10.25,9.25,8.25,7.25)

  • Larger $35m initial commitment
  • Generates substantially more cash early, but annual cash flows decline over time
  • The timing may be valuable because earlier cash flows carry greater present value

Required Financial Analysis

Net Present Value

At the required 10% hurdle rate

Initiative A

PV of future cash flows = $26.13m NPV = 26.13-20 NPVa = $6.13m

Initiative B

PV of future cash flows = $43.76m NPV = 43.67 - 35 NPVb = $8.67m

NPV Decision

B > A

Both initiatives have positive NPVs, so both exceed the required 10% return.

However, initiative B creates approximately:

8.67-6.13 = $2.54m

more economic value

Internal Rate of Return

The calculated IRRs are:

IRRa = ~19.91% IRRb = ~18.93%

Where both comfortably exceed the 10% hurdle rate.

However, IRRa > IRRb, hence IRR ranks A first while NPV ranks B first.

The Key Conflict

MeasureInitiative AInitiative BFavours
Initial Investment$20m$35m-
NPV @ 10%$6.13m$8.67mB
IRR19.91%18.93%A

Why

  • A produces the higher percentage return on the capital invested
  • B operates at a larger scale and creates more total dollar value
  • IRR can therefore favour the smaller, higher-return investment while NPV favours the investment that adds more absolute economic value

If Gilead must choose between the alternatives and its objectives its shareholder value creation, then we should opt for Initiative B based on NPV, not simply the project with the highest IRR.

Strategic Interpretation

Initiative A - Case for Investment

Financial argument:

  • Smaller 20m commitment
  • Positive $6.13m NPV
  • Higher 19.91% IRR
  • Stable annual cash flows

Strategic argument:

  • Supports customer engagement and digital personalisation
  • Potentially less operationally disruptive than constructing physical fulfilment infrastructure

Potential Management Trap: Claiming A is automatically superior because its IRR is higher

This ignores scale and total value creation

Initiative B - Case for Investment

Financial argument:

  • Creates the greater economic value: $8.67m NPV
  • IRR of 18.93% still substantially exceeds the 10% hurdle rate
  • Receives relatively large cash flows early in the investment life

Strategic argument:

  • Strengthens omni-channel capability
  • Reduces dependence on third-party logistics
  • Could provide infrastructure supporting longer-term digital retail growth

Potential Concern: the $35m commitment and greater operational complexity expose Gilead to more investment risk

Questions to Use Against Management’s Arguments

  1. Are they maximising value or simply maximising a convenient metric?
    1. Higher IRR does not necessarily mean greater value
  2. Are they considering the scale of the investment?
    1. A smaller project can generate a higher percentage return while creating fewer dollars of value
  3. Are they considering cash-flow timing?
    1. Early cash flows are economically more valuable than equivalent later cash flows
  4. Are they appropriately incorporating risk?
    1. The case describes different implementation risks despite applying the same 10% hurdle rate
  5. Are strategic claims supported by the cash-flow assumptions?
    1. Strong strategic narratives do not compensate for weak economics
  6. Is the recommendation consistent with Gilead’s objective?
    1. If the objective is economic value creation, NPV should carry greater weight than IRR or accounting measures

Excel Checks

Initiative A

=NPV(10%,6,6,6,6,6,6)-20

Result: $6.13m

=IRR(\{-20,6,6,6,6,6,6\})

Result: 19.91%

Initiative B

=NPV(10%,12.25,11.25,10.25,9.25,8.25,7.25)-35

Result: $8.67m

=IRR(\{-35,12.25,11.25,10.25,9.25,8.25,7.25\})

Result: 18.93%

Analytical Takeaways

  • Both initiatives are financially acceptable at a 10% hurdle rate, but that does not make them equally attractive
  • The central conflict is deliberate: A has the higher IRR, while B has the higher NPV
  • If choosing between the two economic value creation, B is financially preferred because it creates approximately $2.54m more present value
  • The strongest class discussion point is likely not simply which number is larger, but whether management is using the correct decision criterion and whether the same hurdle rate appropriately reflects the project’s different risks
