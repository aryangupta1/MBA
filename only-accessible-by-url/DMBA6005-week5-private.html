Global Green Books — Class Prep Cheat Sheet

1. Case Overview

1.1 Project Objective

Global Green Books is launching a sustainability-focused publication collection supported by:

  • Social media content
  • Paid advertising
  • Influencer engagement
  • Live launch event
  • Livestreaming
  • Reporting and analytics

The campaign needs to be high-impact but cost-effective because the organisation is already under budget pressure.

1.2 Existing Management Problems

  • Influencer costs historically become known too late.
  • Venue/equipment deadlines have been missed, creating last-minute surcharges.
  • Designers and videographers are pulled into competing projects.
  • Resource availability makes budgets difficult to finalise.
  • Creative work, suppliers and the fixed event date create significant schedule dependencies.

Core issue: This is not simply a cost-estimation problem; it is a cost + resource + schedule dependency problem.

4. Target Profit Margin

The project has a 20% target profit margin.

Profit Margin = Profit / Revenue

Therefore:

Required Revenue = Cost / (1 − Profit Margin)

Using the contingency-adjusted cost:

$12,510 / 0.80 = $15,637.50

Therefore:

  • Required revenue ≈ $15,638
  • Target profit ≈ $3,128
  • Profit margin = 20%

4.1 Margin vs Markup

Do not calculate:

$12,510 × 1.20 = $15,012

This represents a 20% markup on cost, not a 20% profit margin.

At $15,012 revenue:

Profit margin = $2,502 / $15,012 ≈ 16.7%

5.2 AI Tools & Consumables

This item is ambiguous:

  • Campaign-specific consumables → Direct
  • Shared AI subscriptions/tools → Indirect

5.3 Why the Distinction Matters

  • Direct costs show resources specifically consumed by the campaign.
  • Indirect costs capture supporting organisational resources.
  • Ignoring indirect costs can make a project appear artificially cheap.

Key principle: Budgeting should reflect the project's true economic resource consumption, not just its visible invoices.

6. Mid-Campaign Diagnosis

At the halfway point:

  • 50% of available time has elapsed
  • Only 30% of campaign assets are completed
  • Completed assets are not yet live
  • Planning is approximately 50% complete
  • Venue deposit remains unpaid
  • One contractor has dropped out
  • Video editing took twice as long as planned
  • Event date remains fixed in two weeks

6.1 Main Warning Sign

50% time elapsed + 30% assets completed + 0% live + fixed deadline

The remaining work must now be delivered faster than originally planned.

Likely consequences:

  • Overtime
  • Expedited suppliers
  • Replacement contractors
  • Rushed approvals
  • Premium bookings
  • Greater rework risk

Key relationship:

Schedule delay → fewer delivery options → higher recovery costs

7. Known Cost Variance

Editor budget:

8 hrs × $50 = $400

Actual editing effort:

16 hrs × $50 = $800

Therefore:

Cost variance = +$400

The initial contingency does not specifically address creative rework, revealing a weakness in the original risk allowance.

8.1 Cost–Schedule Coupling

In fast-moving projects, schedule and cost are interconnected.

A delay can increase costs even when project scope has not changed because the team may need to:

  • add resources;
  • pay overtime;
  • use more expensive suppliers;
  • accelerate approvals;
  • compress downstream activities.

9. Contingency Budgeting

9.1 Purpose

Contingency provides a financial buffer for identified uncertainty.

Current allowances cover:

  • Influencer negotiation risk → 15%
  • Event logistics risk → 10%

9.2 Is the Current Contingency Appropriate?

The realised risks are now:

  • Contractor failure
  • Creative rework
  • Resource availability
  • Schedule compression

These do not closely match the original contingency categories.

Recommendation: Reassess the allocation of contingency rather than simply increasing every budget line.

Additional contingency should focus on:

  • Replacement contractors
  • Extra creative labour
  • Rework
  • Schedule recovery

Key principle: Contingency should reflect the project's actual risk exposure, not be a generic percentage added to cost.

10. Responding to a Fixed Event Date

10.1 Constraint Priority

Because the event date is fixed, schedule becomes the dominant constraint.

The team should:

  1. Identify must-have campaign deliverables.
  2. Remove or defer lower-value content.
  3. Identify critical dependencies.
  4. Reallocate resources to critical activities.
  5. Replace the failed contractor where necessary.
  6. Lock approvals to prevent further revision cycles.
  7. Secure the venue immediately.

10.2 Agile Response

If time and budget are constrained, scope should flex.

Instead of attempting every planned asset, prioritise:

  • Core launch video
  • Highest-value social content
  • Influencer content
  • Essential event promotion

Protect the outcome, not every original deliverable.

11. Useful Project Management Tools

11.1 Work Breakdown Structure (WBS)

Use the WBS to identify:

  • Remaining tasks
  • Ownership
  • Dependencies
  • Estimated cost
  • Completion status

11.2 Critical Path / Dependency Analysis

Identify activities that directly threaten the fixed launch date.

Example:

Creative production → Approval → Scheduling → Publication → Promotion → Event

A delay early in this chain can affect every downstream activity.

11.3 Rolling Forecast

Replace reliance on the original budget with:

Original Budget → Actual Cost to Date → Estimate to Complete → Forecast Final Cost

11.4 Risk Register

Track:

  • Risk
  • Probability
  • Impact
  • Owner
  • Mitigation
  • Contingency

11.5 Scope Prioritisation

Use:

  • Must have
  • Should have
  • Could have

to protect critical value when time becomes constrained.

12.2 Main Cost-Variance Drivers

The largest planned cost is not necessarily the greatest risk.

Important variance drivers include:

  • Labour hours
  • Revisions and rework
  • Contractor availability
  • External supplier pricing
  • Venue booking timing
  • Internal resource conflicts

Key distinction:

Largest cost ≠ greatest cost risk

13. Improving Cost Forecasting

13.1 Estimate from the WBS

Estimate costs at task/work-package level rather than relying on a single campaign-wide estimate.

13.2 Track Labour Variance

Compare:

Budgeted hours → Actual hours → Forecast remaining hours

This would have identified the editing overrun early.

13.3 Lock External Costs Earlier

Secure quotes/contracts for:

  • Influencers
  • Venue
  • Livestream crew
  • External creative contractors

13.4 Confirm Internal Resource Capacity

Ensure designers, editors and other internal staff are genuinely available before committing to the schedule.

13.5 Use Historical Information

Previous campaigns already demonstrated:

  • Late booking costs
  • Influencer pricing uncertainty
  • Internal resource conflicts

Recurring issues should become forecast assumptions and risk allowances, not future surprises.

14. Weekly Sponsor Reporting

Reporting should be short, decision-oriented and forward-looking.

AreaKey Question
ScheduleAre we ahead, on or behind plan?
ScopeWhat has been delivered and what remains?
CostBudget vs actual vs forecast final cost?
RisksWhat could materially affect delivery?
IssuesWhat has already occurred?
DecisionsWhat requires sponsor approval?
Next WeekWhat are the critical upcoming activities?

14.1 Example Status Report

Schedule: RED

  • 50% of time elapsed
  • 30% of assets completed
  • Assets not yet live

Budget: AMBER

  • Editing effort has doubled
  • Replacement contractor may create further variance

Critical Issue

  • Venue deposit remains unpaid

Decision Required

  • Approve scope prioritisation
  • Secure replacement contractor
  • Finalise venue payment

14.2 Reporting Principle

Poor reporting:

“We spent $X this week.”

Better reporting:

“We have spent $X, expect another $Y to complete the remaining work, and therefore forecast a final project cost of $Z.”

Sponsor reporting should focus on the forecast final outcome, not merely historical expenditure.

15. What Can and Cannot Be Calculated

15.1 Can Calculate

  • Baseline labour cost
  • Overhead
  • Starting project budget
  • Contingency under stated assumptions
  • Known editor cost variance
  • Revenue required for the target profit margin

15.2 Cannot Reliably Calculate

  • Final project cost
  • ROI
  • Earned Value
  • Cost Performance Index (CPI)
  • Schedule Performance Index (SPI)

The case does not provide sufficient information on:

  • Actual total spending to date
  • Budgeted value of completed work
  • Remaining labour requirements
  • Replacement contractor cost
  • Expected campaign revenue/benefits

Do not manufacture precision where the underlying data does not exist.

16. Recommended Management Response

The original $12,510 contingency-adjusted estimate should no longer be treated as the operative forecast.

At the halfway point:

  • Schedule performance is weak.
  • Labour cost variance has emerged.
  • A contractor has failed.
  • The venue remains unsecured.
  • The launch date is fixed.

Management should therefore:

  1. Reforecast the cost to completion.
  2. Secure critical external dependencies immediately.
  3. Prioritise must-have campaign assets.
  4. Flex lower-value scope to protect the launch date.
  5. Reallocate contingency toward the risks actually materialising.
  6. Report forecast cost and schedule outcomes weekly to the sponsor.

Analytical Takeaways

  • The starting budget becomes less useful once actual performance diverges from assumptions; management needs a rolling forecast, not a defence of the original estimate.
  • Schedule slippage can become a direct cost problem through overtime, replacement suppliers, expedited work and reduced negotiating power.
  • Contingency should correspond to specific identified risks rather than operate as a generic financial cushion.
  • When the event date is fixed, the most rational Agile response is often to flex lower-value scope rather than attempt to keep time, cost and scope simultaneously fixed.
  • Project reporting should emphasise forecast final cost, delivery risk and decisions required, rather than simply reporting money already spent.
