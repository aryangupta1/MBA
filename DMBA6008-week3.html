Investment evaluation tools

A dollar promised later is worth less than a dollar held now, and that single idea decides which investment wins. Week 3 discounts future cash flows back to today, then sets net present value against total profit, average return on assets, payback and the internal rate of return — and works through where each of the others breaks.

01 / The core idea

What present value converts

Present value (PV) converts a future cash flow into its equivalent value today. A future dollar is worth less than a dollar today because capital has a required return, or cost of capital. The appropriate discount rate represents the return required for committing capital to the investment.

❓

Are the expected future cash flows worth more today than the capital required to obtain them?

02 / Discounting

One year’s cash flow, restated in today’s terms

For a cash flow received in one year, the notes define PV as the value of the future cash flow today and r as the appropriate discount rate, or cost of capital. At a cost of capital of 10%, the future $112,000 is economically equivalent to $101,818 today.

Higher discount rate → lower present value, because investors require greater compensation for supplying capital.

03 / The benchmark

Required return, not the raw cash inflow

An investment creates value only when its return exceeds the return required by its providers of capital. If $100,000 of capital costs 10%, it must grow to $110,000 after one year simply to satisfy the required return. If the investment instead generates $112,000, the $2000 excess represents value created in future-dollar terms.

The one-year comparison Amount
Capital supplied today $100,000
Required after one year at 10% $110,000
Cash the investment generates $112,000
Excess, in future-dollar terms $2000
04 / Today’s dollars

The future surplus is not the value created

The $2000 future surplus must itself be discounted.

$2000/1.10 = $1818

The investment therefore creates $1818 of value today. The distinction matters because the future surplus and today’s value creation are not the same amount.

05 / Net present value

NPV as the measure of value created

NPV = Present Value of Future Cash Flows - Initial Outlay NPV = $101818 - $100000 = $1818

The initial investment is therefore not compared directly with the nominal future cash flow; the future cash flow must first be expressed in today’s terms. NPV provides a dollar measure of the economic value generated by an investment, forming a fundamental basis for valuation and investment decision-making.

Analytical takeaways

  • Investment performance should be assessed against the cost of capital, not simply whether the future cash inflow exceeds the initial expenditure.
  • A positive accounting or cash gain does not by itself establish value creation; the time value of money must be incorporated.
💡

NPV translates the answer into today’s dollars, allowing managers to judge whether an investment genuinely increases economic value.

06 / Decision context

Comparing mutually exclusive investments

Mutually exclusive projects compete for the same decision: selecting one means rejecting the other. Where the alternatives deliver the same strategic or service outcome, appraisal is not asked which project looks best on its own, but which of them creates the greatest economic value — and that is not necessarily the one reporting the highest accounting profit.

This requires separating three possible performance measures:

  • Total accounting profit
  • Average return on assets
  • Net Present Value (NPV)
07 / Profit versus cash flow

Depreciation creates an important difference

Depreciation is an accounting expense, but it is not a current cash outflow. Profit is therefore reported after a charge that no money left the business to pay, and adding that charge back recovers the cash the project actually generated:

Cash Flow = Profit + Depreciation

Where depreciation is the relevant non-cash expense and taxes are excluded.

A project can therefore report an accounting loss while still generating positive cash flow. For investment valuation the relevant measure is the cash generated, because value ultimately depends on the cash available to investors rather than on accounting earnings.

Why profit can mislead investment decisions

Accounting profit does not directly incorporate the three drivers of value:

Cash flow + timing of cash flow + risk

Therefore, ranking investments purely by total profit can produce a different decisions from ranking them by economic value.

08 / Average return on assets

Average ROA as an appraisal measure

The second of the three measures. Its calculation, and the illustration for a fully depreciated asset under straight-line depreciation, are held as images in the source notes and are not reproduced on this page.

If an asset falls from $33m to zero:

(33+0)/2 = $16.m

Limitation

  • Average ROA remains an accounting-based return mesaure
  • It can favour a project with higher accounting profits even where another project generates cash earlier, which may make that alternative economically more value
  • It therefore does not fully incorporate cash flow timing or risk
09 / The preferred measure

Why NPV gives a different decision

Building on from the Concept of Present Value, NPV evaluates projects using their actual cash-flow pattern rather than accounting profit.

For mutually exclusive projects:

💡

Prefer the project with the highest positive NPV, assuming the projects are comparable and the correct hurdle rate has been used

Set side by side, the three measures differ in what they take into account:

Measure Basis What the notes say about it
Total accounting profit Accounting earnings Does not directly incorporate cash flow, timing of cash flow or risk
Average return on assets An accounting-based return mesaure Does not fully incorporate cash flow timing or risk
Net Present Value The project's actual cash-flow pattern Depends on the required rate of return applied to those cash flows
10 / Hurdle rate and ranking

Risk determines the required return

The Hurdle Rate / WACC / cost of capital represents the minimum return given the project's risk:

  • Higher perceived risk → higher hurdle rate
  • Lower perceived risk → lower hurdle rate

The choice of hurdle rate can therefore change which mutually exclusive project is preferred.

Why cash-flow timing matters more at higher rates

Project B receives a greater proportion of its cash earlier. As the hurdle rate increases:

  • Distant cash flows are discounted more heavily
  • Early cash flows retain more of their present value
  • Projects that generate cash sooner become relatively more attractive

Example

Hurdle Rate Preferred Project Interpretation
7% Project A Lower discounting increases the relative value of its later cash flows
10% Indifferent Both product the same NPV
14% Project B Earlier cash flows become relatively more valuable

At 14%, project A has a negative NPV, meaning it fails to earn the required return and destroys value, while project B remains positive.

💡

Different cash-flow patterns can have the same economic value at one discount rate

11 / NPV sensitivity

Project choice is not independent of the discount rate

The ranking an appraisal produces is a joint property of the cash flows and the rate applied to them, which is why the rate cannot be treated as a background assumption:

  • NPV is not simply a property of the project's cash flows; it also depends on the required rate of return applied to those cash flows
  • Two projects can switch rankings when the hurdle rate changes because their cash-flow timing differs
  • Determining the appropriate hurdle rate is therefore a critical part of investment appraisal, not merely a mechanical input

Managerial implication

A project should not automatically be labelled superior because it has:

  • Higher total profits
  • Higher accounting returns
  • A higher NPV calculated using an inadequately justified discount rate
💡

Management must assess whether the hurdle rate properly reflects the risk of investment

12 / Excel NPV

An important calculation detail

Excel's NPV() function discounts future cash flows only. The two worked illustrations of this behaviour are held as images in the source notes and are not reproduced on this page. The initial investment occurs at time 0 and must therefore be added separately, where CF0 is normally negative.

🚨

Common Error: Including the time-0 investment inside Excel's NPV() function would incorrectly discount a cash flow that already occurs today

13 / Analytical takeaways

What to carry out of investment appraisal

  • For mutually exclusive investments, the central question is which project creates the greatest value after considering cash flow, timing and risk, not which reports the highest profit
  • Accounting measures such as profit and average ROA can conflict with NPV because they do not adequately capture the timing of cash flows
  • Projects with earlier cash flows become relatively more attractive as the hurdle rate rises
  • A positive NPV is only as credible as the risk assessment underlying the hurdle rate

Managers should therefore challenge both sides of an appraisal:

❓

Are the cash-flow forecasts credible, and is the discount rate appropriate for their risk?

14 / The core idea

The rate that drives NPV to zero

IRR is the discount rate that makes an investment’s NPV equal to zero. It represents the investment’s implied annual return, set by the cash flows generated and by the timing of those cash flows. Unlike accounting ROA, it is a cash-flow-based measure rather than a profit-based one.

NPV = 0 at r = IRR

Read forward rather than backward, IRR is the rate at which the investment is effectively growing each year as its cash flows occur and compound. For a standalone investment it is judged against the hurdle rate.

💡

This provides an intuitive percentage return that can be compared with the required return.

15 / Against other measures

IRR, accounting return and NPV

Three measures of the same investment, separated by what they are built from and by whether they recognise when the money arrives.

Measure Based on Timing recognised? Primary interpretation
Accounting ROA Profit and asset values No Accounting profitability
IRR Cash flows Yes Investment’s implied return
NPV Cash flows discounted at required return Yes Dollar value created

IRR improves on accounting return by incorporating cash and timing. It does not, however, directly measure the amount of economic value created — that is what NPV reports.

16 / Ranking conflict

IRR and mutually exclusive projects

Building on from investment appraisals, Project A has an IRR of 13.8% and Project B an IRR of 14.6%. Using IRR alone would therefore rank Project B above Project A. NPV analysis does not settle on the same order:

  • At some hurdle rates, project A is preferred
  • At other rates, project B is preferred
  • At 10%, their NPVs are equal

IRR is useful for evaluating a single investment, particularly when management wants an intuitive percentage return. It becomes less reliable when comparing mutually exclusive investments, because different cash-flow patterns can cause IRR and NPV to produce conflicting rankings.

💡

When IRR and NPV conflict, prefer NPV.

17 / In Excel

Calculating IRR from the cash flows

Excel calculates IRR using all project cash flows, including the initial time-0 outlay.

=IFR(CF0:CFn)
18 / Takeaways

Why IRR needs caution

IRR is popular because a percentage return is easy to communicate and compare with a hurdle rate. But its interaction and underlying mathematics can create problems when comparing alternative projects.

💡

IRR should be treated as a supporting appraisal measure rather than the primary value creation criterion for mutually exclusive investment.

Analytical takeaways

  • IRR answers “what percentage return does this investment generate?”
  • NPV answers “how much value does it create?”
  • A higher IRR does not automatically mean a mutually exclusive investment is economically superior.
  • IRR is most useful for standalone investment assessment; project ranking should rely primarily on NPV.
  • Managers should ask whether an attractive IRR also corresponds to the highest value creation at the relevant hurdle rate.
19 / Evaluation criteria

What a sound appraisal method has to capture

My notes set the test before naming any method: a sound appraisal method should it captures the three drivers of value.

Cash flow + timing of cash flow + risk
  • Cash flow: value depends on cash generated, not accounting profit
  • Timing: earlier cash flows are more valuable than later cash flows
  • Risk: uncertain cash flows require a higher required return
💡

NPV satisfies all three criteria, because it discounts future cash flows at a hurdle rate reflecting risk

Everything that follows is that same test applied to three alternatives.

20 / Payback period

What payback measures, and what it leaves out

Payback period measures how long it takes to recover the initial investment from future cash inflows. It is popular because it is quick and easy to calculate.

My notes gather its problems into one table. The first row records a strength rather than a problem, and is reproduced here as written.

Issue Why it matters
Recognises cash flow Useful starting point
Ignores timing within the payback period Treats early and late cash flows as equivalent
Ignores risk Cannot distinguish investments with different uncertainty
Does not measure value creation A project can repay its investment yet still have negative NPV

22 / Average accounting ROA

Why average ROA is weak for investment appraisal

The definition and calculation of average accounting ROA are held as an image in the source notes and are not reproduced on this page. What my notes state in words is why it is weak as an appraisal measure, on three grounds:

  • It uses accounting profit rather than cash flow
  • Averaging removes information about when returns occur
  • It does not explicitly incorporate risk

Averaging is particularly problematic in finance because timing matters

💡

Average ROA may describe accounting performance, but it should not determine which investment creates the most value

23 / Internal rate of return

What IRR captures, and where it stops

IRR reaches further than payback or an accounting average, but not all the way to the third criterion.

  • Incorporates cash flow
  • Incorporates timing of cash flow
  • Does not incorporate project risk on its own

The IRR must therefore be compared against an independently estimated hurdle rate.

Reinvestment assumption

  • IRR is commonly interpreted as assuming interim cash flows are reinvested at the project’s IRR
  • This may be unrealistic, particularly where the calculated IRR is very high

Scale problem

IRR measures a percentage return, so it can favour a smaller project with a high growth rate even when a larger project creates more dollar value.

Investment Outlay Year 1 cash flow IRR NPV at 10%
X 20m 40m 100% 16.4m
Y 40m 70m 75% 23.6m
  • IRR ranks X first because 100% > 75%
  • NPV ranks Y first because it creates more economic value
💡

IRR ignores the size of the investment base when comparing projects

24 / Why NPV is preferred

The measure that meets all three criteria

For investment selection, the preferred approach is NPV because it:

  • uses actual cash flows
  • recognises when those cash flows occur
  • incorporates risk through the hurdle rate
  • measures value creation in dollar terms
  • avoids misleading ranking caused by payback, accounting averages or percentage-return measures

For mutually exclusive projects:

💡

Choose the investment with the highest positive NPV, using an appropriately estimated hurdle rate

25 / Analytical takeaways

What to carry out of the common approaches

  • A quick or intuitive appraisal measure is not necessarily a good decision rule
  • Payback can hide value destruction, average ROA ignores the economics of cash timing, and IRR can favour a high-return but smaller-value project
  • Percentage returns should not be confused with dollar value creation
❓

Which alternative creates the greatest present value after considering cash flow, timing and risk?

💡

Where common appraisals measure conflict, rely on NPV
